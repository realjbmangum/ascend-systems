# Impeccable — Ascend Systems

## Design Context

### Users

Two audiences, both arriving with a job in hand rather than browsing.

**Partners** — subcontractors, agencies, chambers, event organisers and referral partners who need the Ascend mark for their own materials: a deck, a sponsor banner, a co-branded one-pager, a printed program. They arrive from a link Brian sent them, they want the file, and they will spend under two minutes on the page. If they cannot find a usable download fast, they will right-click the logo off the marketing site instead and ship it at the wrong size on the wrong background.

**Clients and prospects** — people who met Brian at a Chamber or BNI meeting and are checking whether Ascend Systems is a real firm. They are not looking for files. They are looking for evidence of seriousness. For them the page is a credibility artifact, and the fact that a brand standard exists at all does most of the work.

The page has to serve both without compromising either: fast, obvious asset retrieval for the first, visible rigour for the second.

### Brand Personality

From `brand-kit.md`, in Brian's own words:

- **Confident** — dark palette, bold typography. "We don't shout — we command."
- **Modern** — AI-forward, 2026 energy, not hiding from technology.
- **Warm** — burnt orange keeps it human. Brian is the face. Handshakes matter.
- **Systematic** — "Systems" is in the name. Everything is built with precision.
- **Trustworthy** — dark charcoal reads as authority. The brand should look like it has been here for years.

Voice is plain and specific. Words the brand uses: handled, covered, managed, built, running, automated. Words it does not: leverage, synergy, ecosystem, paradigm, disrupt, revolutionary, cutting-edge, game-changing.

**Naming rule, confirmed by Brian 31 Jul 2026: always "Ascend Systems", never "Ascend" alone.**

### Aesthetic Direction

**Theme: dark.** Not a default — derived. The brand kit specifies charcoal as the authority signal and the primary logo application is white-and-orange on charcoal `#1E2A32`. A light brand page would misrepresent the brand it documents.

The reference object is a **type foundry specimen sheet or an engineering spec card**: precise, generously spaced, systematic, technical values set in monospace. This suits a company with "Systems" in its name and gives the page its own character without inventing a second visual language.

Anti-references: SaaS marketing page, endless identical feature cards, decorative iconography, anything that looks like a template.

### Design Principles

1. **The tokens are read, never retyped.** `src/styles/globals.css` is the single source of truth for colour. The brand page parses it at build time and renders what it finds. A brand page that hardcodes hex values will drift from the site exactly as `portfolio.ts` drifted from the PottyDirectory case study — and a partner shipping the wrong orange is the specific failure this page exists to prevent.
2. **Documentation beats decoration.** Every element on the page is a fact a partner needs: a file, a value, a rule, a boundary. Nothing is present to look designed.
3. **The asset is the hero.** The mark gets room to breathe and is shown on the backgrounds it will actually be used on. Downloads are never more than one click from the thing they depict.
4. **Rules are stated as boundaries, not preferences.** "Never recolour the mark", not "we prefer the mark in its original colours." Partners follow instructions, not suggestions.
5. **Show, then constrain.** Correct usage appears before prohibited usage. Lead with what to do.

## Deliberate deviations from impeccable defaults

**Inter is used, despite being on the reflex-font reject list.** Inter is Ascend Systems' actual specified brand typeface — in `brand-kit.md` and as `--font-sans` in `globals.css`, and it is what the live site renders. This page documents the brand; substituting a more distinctive face would make the page factually wrong about its own subject. The font ban exists to prevent monoculture in greenfield design, which is not what this is. JetBrains Mono is likewise the brand's specified monospace and is used for technical values, which is its documented role in the kit rather than generic "technical vibes" styling.

## Known brand-source conflict, resolved 31 Jul 2026

`brand-kit.md`'s Logo Direction section described a mark that does not match the shipped artwork. It forbade mountains and limited the logo to two colours, while the actual mark is two charcoal peaks with an orange chevron and ascending arrow, a white cross at centre, and a light wide-tracked wordmark rather than the specified Inter ExtraBold at `-0.03em`.

**Brian's decision: the shipped logo is authoritative.** `brand-kit.md` was written before the mark existed and was never reconciled. The kit gets corrected to describe reality; the brand page documents the real mark.

**The cross is shown without comment.** It is part of the mark and may not be altered or removed. The page does not explain it, and copy never references it.
