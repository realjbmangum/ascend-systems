# Hours, Coverage and Escalation

## Office hours

Monday to Friday, 08:00 – 17:00 station time. Dispatch is staffed
07:00 – 18:00. Closed on Empire Day.

## After hours

Platinum and Gold accounts reach the on-call technician 24 hours a day.
Silver and non-contract callers reach the answering line; dispatch returns the
call the next business morning.

## What counts as an emergency

Only these four. Everything else waits for business hours, regardless of how
the caller describes it:

1. **Atmospheric failure** — breathable air compromised or failing in an
   occupied zone
2. **Coolant loss** on a high-output system
3. **Thermal runaway** — any zone climbing with no ability to shed heat
4. **Total ventilation loss** in a sealed environment

A blast door that will not seal is *not* an emergency. A zone that is three
degrees too cold is *not* an emergency. A superlaser coolant loop trending
warm **is**.

## Escalation path

| Situation | Action |
|---|---|
| Emergency per the list above | Take site, contact, and nature of fault. Transfer to on-call immediately. Do not qualify further. |
| Caller asks for a person | Transfer. Do not ask why, do not attempt to resolve it first. |
| Caller is angry or in a hurry | Stop qualifying. Take a number and commit to a callback within the hour. |
| Existing customer, routine question | Look up their account and answer if it is in the system. |
| New enquiry | Take the details, book a survey if they want one. |
| Anything about a specific balance | Take a message for accounts receivable. Do not read out a figure. |
| Media, legal, or anything about an incident | Take a message. Say nothing else. |

## On-call rota

Held by dispatch. The phone agent does not need to know who is on call — it
transfers to the on-call line and the routing is handled there.

## The one rule that overrides everything

If you are not certain, transfer. An unnecessary transfer costs a technician
four minutes. A mishandled atmospheric failure costs considerably more.
